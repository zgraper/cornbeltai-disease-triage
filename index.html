Crop Disease & Pest Identification Guide
Top 15 diseases & pests — Corn and Soybeans
🌽 Corn — Diseases
1
Gray Leaf Spot
Cercospora zeae-maydis
Disease
🖼️ Field Symptom corn/gray-leaf-spot-1
🖼️ Close-up Lesion corn/gray-leaf-spot-2
Fungal disease favored by warm, humid conditions. One of the most yield-limiting foliar diseases in corn across the Corn Belt.
Look for: Long, rectangular, tan-to-gray lesions with distinct parallel edges running between leaf veins. Lesions may coalesce to blight entire leaves.
2
Northern Corn Leaf Blight
Exserohilum turcicum
Disease
🖼️ Field Symptom corn/nclb-1
🖼️ Spore Detail corn/nclb-2
Common fungal leaf blight favored by moderate temperatures and extended wet periods. Causes significant yield loss when infection occurs before or at silking.
Look for: Large (1–6 in.), cigar-shaped, grayish-green to tan lesions on leaves. Dark, sooty spore masses may appear within lesions under humid conditions.
3
Common Rust
Puccinia sorghi
Disease
🖼️ Upper Leaf Surface corn/common-rust-1
🖼️ Pustule Close-up corn/common-rust-2
Foliar fungal disease spread by windborne spores. Most damaging when infection occurs early and on susceptible hybrids.
Look for: Small, powdery, brick-red to cinnamon-brown pustules scattered on both upper and lower leaf surfaces. Pustules turn dark brown-black late in the season.
4
Tar Spot
Phyllachora maydis
Disease
🖼️ Leaf Stromata corn/tar-spot-1
🖼️ Fish-eye Lesion corn/tar-spot-2
Rapidly emerging disease across the Midwest. Can cause severe yield losses (up to 50+ bu/ac) in susceptible hybrids during favorable cool, humid conditions.
Look for: Small, raised, shiny black spots (stromata) that cannot be rubbed off. "Fish-eye" lesions have a tan halo surrounding a dark center.
5
Goss's Wilt
Clavibacter michiganensis subsp. nebraskensis
Disease
🖼️ Leaf Blight corn/goss-wilt-1
🖼️ Bacterial Freckles corn/goss-wilt-2
Bacterial disease spread by hail, wind-driven rain, and leaf-feeding insects. Most damaging when infection occurs early in the season.
Look for: Water-soaked, pale green to tan streaks running the length of leaves. Shiny, dried bacterial exudate ("freckles") within lesions is diagnostic.
6
Gibberella Ear Rot
Fusarium graminearum
Disease
🖼️ Pink/Red Mold corn/gibberella-ear-rot-1
🖼️ Kernel Damage corn/gibberella-ear-rot-2
Fungal ear disease that can produce mycotoxins (deoxynivalenol/DON and zearalenone) harmful to livestock. Favored by wet conditions at silking and grain fill.
Look for: Pink to reddish-white mold starting at the tip of the ear and progressing downward. Infected kernels may be lightweight and shriveled.
7
Stalk Rot Complex
Fusarium spp. / Gibberella zeae
Disease
🖼️ Stalk Cross-Section corn/stalk-rot-1
🖼️ Lodged Plants corn/stalk-rot-2
Multiple fungal pathogens cause internal stalk decay, leading to premature death and lodging. Plants stressed by foliar disease, drought, or compaction are most vulnerable.
Look for: Lower internodes that are soft and discolored. The "push test" — a plant that falls when pushed — indicates stalk rot. Pith tissue is shredded or hollow.
8
Southern Rust
Puccinia polysora
Disease
🖼️ Orange Pustules corn/southern-rust-1
🖼️ Upper Leaf Surface corn/southern-rust-2
More aggressive than common rust. Spores migrate north from the Gulf each season. Epidemics during grain fill can cause significant yield loss if not managed with fungicide.
Look for: Small, densely packed, bright orange to tan pustules concentrated mainly on the upper leaf surface. Lesions are more circular than common rust.
🐛 Corn — Pests
9
Corn Rootworm
Diabrotica virgifera virgifera / D. barberi
Pest
🖼️ Root Pruning corn/corn-rootworm-1
🖼️ Adult Beetle corn/corn-rootworm-2
The most economically damaging pest of corn in North America. Larval root feeding leads to lodging, and adult silk clipping can affect pollination.
Look for: Goose-necking (bent lower stalks), root masses pruned to the base, and plants that pull up easily. Adult beetles are yellow-green with black stripes.
10
European Corn Borer
Ostrinia nubilalis
Pest
🖼️ Stalk Entry Holes corn/ecb-1
🖼️ Larva in Stalk corn/ecb-2
Historically one of the most damaging corn pests. Bt hybrids have significantly reduced impact, but populations persist in non-Bt fields and field edges.
Look for: "Shot-hole" feeding on whorl leaves, sawdust-like frass at entry holes, broken tassels, and stalk tunneling that can cause lodging.
11
Corn Earworm
Helicoverpa zea
Pest
🖼️ Tip Damage corn/corn-earworm-1
🖼️ Caterpillar on Ear corn/corn-earworm-2
Primary pest of the ear. Larvae enter through the silk channel and feed on kernels at the tip. Also vectors ear molds and mycotoxins.
Look for: Frass and damaged tip kernels when husks are peeled. Caterpillars are cream to tan with brown stripes and range from ½–2 inches long.
12
Western Bean Cutworm
Striacosta albicosta
Pest
🖼️ Egg Mass on Leaf corn/wbc-1
🖼️ Larva in Ear corn/wbc-2
Range has expanded eastward into the Corn Belt. Early larval feeding occurs on anthers and silk; late feeding damages kernels and promotes mold development.
Look for: Dome-shaped egg masses on upper leaf surface near the ear. Older larvae feed inside the husk; look for frass and multiple larvae per ear.
13
Black Cutworm
Agrotis ipsilon
Pest
🖼️ Cut Plants corn/black-cutworm-1
🖼️ Larva ID corn/black-cutworm-2
Migratory pest that can cause severe stand loss in early-planted corn. Larvae cut through stems at or below the soil surface. Larvae from moths arriving in April–May cause the greatest injury.
Look for: Seedling wilting, completely cut plants lying on the soil, and larvae (dark gray, greasy-looking) found just below the surface near damaged plants.
14
Corn Leaf Aphid
Rhopalosiphum maidis
Pest
🖼️ Tassel Colony corn/corn-aphid-1
🖼️ Honeydew Sooty Mold corn/corn-aphid-2
Can build to very high populations quickly in tassels and upper leaves. Excess honeydew promotes sooty mold and interferes with photosynthesis. Also transmits Barley Yellow Dwarf Virus.
Look for: Dense, blue-green aphid colonies in the tassel and on upper leaf surfaces. Sticky honeydew and black sooty mold are secondary indicators.
15
Silk Beetle / Corn Silk Fly
Contarinia sorghicola / Isosorbide
Pest
🖼️ Silk Feeding Damage corn/silk-beetle-1
🖼️ Tip Fill Gaps corn/silk-beetle-2
Several beetle species (including Japanese beetles) feed heavily on corn silks during pollination. Heavy feeding at emergence before pollination is complete can reduce kernel set significantly.
Look for: Silks clipped to less than ½ inch before 50% pollen shed is complete. Poor tip fill and incomplete kernel rows can indicate silk feeding during pollination.

🫘 Soybeans — Diseases
1
Sudden Death Syndrome
Fusarium virguliforme
Disease
🖼️ Foliar Symptoms soybeans/sds-1
🖼️ Root Rot soybeans/sds-2
One of the most yield-limiting soybean diseases. Root infection occurs early, but foliar symptoms appear during reproductive stages when toxins move from roots to leaves.
Look for: Interveinal chlorosis and necrosis on upper leaves. Leaves die and fall, leaving petioles attached. Roots and lower stem are brown; interior pith remains white (unlike brown stem rot).
2
Soybean Cyst Nematode
Heterodera glycines (SCN)
Disease
🖼️ Cysts on Roots soybeans/scn-1
🖼️ Stunted Patches soybeans/scn-2
The most economically damaging pathogen of soybeans in North America. Infestations often cause little visible above-ground symptoms while silently reducing yields.
Look for: Yellow, stunted circular patches in fields. Tiny white-to-yellow lemon-shaped cysts visible on roots when pulled early season. Soil sampling is the most reliable diagnostic tool.
3
White Mold (Sclerotinia Stem Rot)
Sclerotinia sclerotiorum
Disease
🖼️ Cottony Mycelium soybeans/white-mold-1
🖼️ Sclerotia on Stem soybeans/white-mold-2
Major disease in northern Corn Belt and high-yield environments. Favored by cool, wet conditions during flowering and dense canopies that reduce airflow.
Look for: White, cottony mycelium on stems at or near the soil line. Stems become bleached and shredded. Hard, black, pellet-like sclerotia inside and outside infected stems.
4
Phytophthora Root & Stem Rot
Phytophthora sojae
Disease
🖼️ Brown Stem Lesion soybeans/phytophthora-1
🖼️ Damping-off Seedlings soybeans/phytophthora-2
Favored by saturated soils. Can cause pre-emergence damping-off and post-emergence stem rot. Resistant varieties and seed treatments with mefenoxam are key management tools.
Look for: Dark brown discoloration of the lower stem, progressing upward from the soil line. Leaves may remain attached but turn yellow then brown. Roots are dark and rotted.
5
Brown Stem Rot
Cadophora gregata
Disease
🖼️ Pith Discoloration soybeans/bsr-1
🖼️ Interveinal Chlorosis soybeans/bsr-2
Soil-borne fungal disease that is often confused with SDS. Most damaging in fields with continuous soybean production and cool, wet soil conditions early in the season.
Look for: Brown discoloration of the pith inside the stem (diagnostic; pith is white in SDS). Foliar interveinal chlorosis and necrosis appear similar to SDS during reproductive stages.
6
Frogeye Leaf Spot
Cercospora sojina
Disease
🖼️ Characteristic Lesions soybeans/frogeye-1
🖼️ Lesion Detail soybeans/frogeye-2
Fungal foliar disease that has developed resistance to strobilurin fungicides in some areas. Can defoliate fields and cause seed infection in severe cases.
Look for: Circular to angular lesions with reddish-brown margins and gray to light tan centers on leaves. The "frog eye" appearance — pale center with darker border — is diagnostic.
7
Septoria Brown Spot
Septoria glycines
Disease
🖼️ Lower Canopy Spots soybeans/septoria-1
🖼️ Premature Yellowing soybeans/septoria-2
One of the most common foliar diseases of soybeans. While usually a minor yield reducer, severe outbreaks during early reproductive stages can cause significant defoliation.
Look for: Small, irregular, dark brown spots on lower leaves early in the season. Infected leaves yellow prematurely and drop, starting from the bottom of the canopy upward.
8
Soybean Mosaic Virus
SMV (Potyvirus)
Disease
🖼️ Mosaic Leaf Mottling soybeans/smv-1
🖼️ Stunted Plant soybeans/smv-2
One of the most prevalent soybean viruses worldwide. Transmitted by aphids in a non-persistent manner. Seed transmission also occurs. Early infections cause the greatest yield loss.
Look for: Light and dark green mottling (mosaic) on leaves, leaf puckering, distortion, and rugosity. Infected plants are often stunted with reduced pod set.
🐛 Soybeans — Pests
9
Soybean Aphid
Aphis glycines
Pest
🖼️ Colony on Stem soybeans/soybean-aphid-1
🖼️ Winged Adult soybeans/soybean-aphid-2
The only aphid species that colonizes soybeans in North America. Can reach outbreak levels rapidly. Economic threshold is 250 aphids per plant on 80% of plants, with populations still increasing.
Look for: Small (1–1.5 mm), pale yellow to light green aphids on undersides of leaves and growing points. Honeydew and sooty mold on leaves are secondary signs.
10
Bean Leaf Beetle
Cerotoma trifurcata
Pest
🖼️ Leaf Feeding Holes soybeans/bean-leaf-beetle-1
🖼️ Adult Beetle soybeans/bean-leaf-beetle-2
Early-season adults can cause significant stand loss by clipping cotyledons and stems. Also vectors Bean Pod Mottle Virus. Pod feeding by late-season adults can reduce seed quality.
Look for: Round, clean-edged holes in leaves and cotyledons. Adults are highly variable in color (red, tan, yellow) but typically have 4 black spots and a black triangle at the head.
11
Brown & Green Stink Bugs
Euschistus servus / Chinavia hilaris
Pest
🖼️ Pod Feeding Damage soybeans/stink-bug-1
🖼️ Adult on Pod soybeans/stink-bug-2
Pod feeders that inject enzymes during feeding, causing shriveled and discolored seeds. Damage is most significant during R3–R6. Rising populations across the Midwest in recent years.
Look for: "Staygreen" syndrome — pods and stems remain green while the rest of the plant matures. Shriveled, wrinkled, or yeast-infected seeds inside pods.
12
Two-Spotted Spider Mite
Tetranychus urticae
Pest
🖼️ Stippled Leaves soybeans/spider-mite-1
🖼️ Webbing on Leaflet soybeans/spider-mite-2
Outbreak pest during hot, dry summers. Populations can explode rapidly and cause severe defoliation and yield loss. Pyrethroid insecticides can worsen outbreaks by killing natural enemies.
Look for: Yellow stippling (tiny dots) on upper leaf surface, fine webbing on undersides of leaves. Infested leaves turn bronze, then brown. Begin scouting field edges during drought.
13
Dectes Stem Borer
Dectes texanus
Pest
🖼️ Stem Tunneling soybeans/dectes-1
🖼️ Girdled Stem soybeans/dectes-2
Longhorn beetle whose larvae tunnel through the main stem. Larvae girdle the stem base before overwintering, causing premature plant death and lodging prior to harvest.
Look for: Wilting of a single branch (petiole girdling). Cut stems show a central tunnel packed with frass. Late-season girdling at the base causes premature lodging and harvest losses.
14
Soybean Looper / Green Cloverworm
Chrysodeixis includens / Hypena scabra
Pest
🖼️ Defoliation Pattern soybeans/defoliator-1
🖼️ Caterpillar on Leaf soybeans/defoliator-2
Sporadic but occasionally severe defoliating pests. Economic thresholds are based on percent defoliation: 20% pre-bloom, 15% during R1–R6, and 25% after R6.
Look for: Ragged leaf edges and skeletonized leaves; caterpillars loop when walking. Green cloverworm is bright green with white stripes. Scout for frass and caterpillars under the canopy.
15
Threecornered Alfalfa Hopper
Spissistilus festinus
Pest
🖼️ Stem Girdling soybeans/tah-1
🖼️ Adult Hopper soybeans/tah-2
Primarily a problem in the southern and central Corn Belt. Adults and nymphs girdle stems with their feeding rings, disrupting phloem flow, which can cause yield loss and lodging.
Look for: Ring-shaped feeding scars on the main stem at or below the cotyledon node. Girdled plants may be stunted, yellowed, or lodged. Adults are small, bright green, and wedge-shaped.
